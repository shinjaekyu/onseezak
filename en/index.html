What to pack for liposuction in Korea

Your clinic will give you its own list, so follow that first. These are the things most people are glad they brought.

  • Two compression garments. You wear one while the other dries. Ask your clinic which type and length you need.
  • Loose tops that open at the front. Zips or buttons are much easier than pulling clothes over your head in the first few days.
  • Soft, elastic-waist pants or a long dress. Darker colors are easier to wear over a garment.
  • Slip-on shoes. Bending down to tie laces is uncomfortable after surgery.
  • A plug adapter. Korea uses 220V with Type C and F sockets.
  • Mild detergent for hand washing. You can also buy it at any convenience store in Seoul.
  • Your travel insurance details. Some insurers need to know about recent surgery, so check your policy before you fly.

Questions people ask before they come

How long will I need to wear a compression garment?

Most Korean clinics recommend about 4 weeks. Overseas plastic surgery societies usually say 4–6 weeks, and depending on the area and how much fat is removed, it can be anywhere from 2 weeks to 3 months.

For the first 1–2 weeks, you'll usually wear it almost all day, taking it off only to shower. Your surgeon decides how long you should wear it and when you can stop.

How many garments do I need?

Two. You need to wash it every day, and wearing a garment that's still damp can irritate your skin. With two, you can always wear one while the other dries.

How long does it take to get one made?

Measuring takes about 30 minutes for a full body, not including the consultation. Your garment is ready 5–8 hours later, so you can pick it up the same day.

Can I get it before my surgery, or have it sent to my clinic?

Yes. Come in before your surgery for measurements, then pick it up from our store, or we'll deliver it to your clinic on the day of your surgery.

Can I fly home wearing it?

When you can fly is up to your clinic, so ask them first. Some overseas guidance says short flights may be possible about a week after surgery and long-haul flights after 10–14 days, but this varies a lot.

  • Wear your garment on the plane.
  • Walk the aisle every 1–2 hours and move your ankles while seated.
  • Drink water often, and go easy on alcohol and caffeine.
  • Keep a spare garment in your carry-on.
How do I wash it?

Hand washing is safest. Use lukewarm water and a mild detergent, press the water out with a towel instead of wringing, and dry it flat in the shade.

If you use a washing machine, turn it inside out, put it in a laundry bag and use a delicate cycle. Don't use a dryer, bleach, fabric softener or an iron. Hotel laundry services often use hot water and dryers, so it's better not to send it out.

What if it feels too tight? When should I call my clinic?

It's too tight if your skin turns pale, you feel numbness or tingling, it's hard to breathe, or marks are still there 30 minutes after you take it off. Loosen it by one hook or take it off for a moment. If the color or feeling doesn't come back, contact your clinic.

Call your clinic straight away if one side suddenly swells or hurts much more, you see redness, heat or discharge, you get blisters, or pain isn't controlled by your medication. If you're short of breath, have chest pain, or one leg is swollen and painful, get help immediately — call 119 in Korea if you can't reach your clinic.
